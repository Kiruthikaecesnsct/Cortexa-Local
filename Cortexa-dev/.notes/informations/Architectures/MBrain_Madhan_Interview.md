# MBrain — Interview with Dr. Madhan: How You Actually Evaluate and Build Patents

**Purpose:** This is not a theory questionnaire. It's asking you to describe, in your own words, how you actually do the work — evaluating novelty, spotting a patent opportunity in a pile of technical material, identifying hidden or unrealized ideas in a body of work, generating a new invention concept, deciding what's worth filing, and judging what would make an existing draft stronger. Your answers become the encoded reasoning MBrain runs on. MBrain is being built to *be* the decision layer the Harvesting and Seeding engines act on — the system that calls novelty, flags candidates, reviews patents, and surfaces improvement suggestions on its own, without a person re-checking each verdict. The goal is for MBrain's reasoning to stand in for yours at the point of decision, so describe your thinking in enough detail that it could run as a standing rule, not just as a one-off judgment call. If a question doesn't match how you actually think about the problem, say so instead of forcing an answer — that's useful too.

**How to fill this in:** Each question has a suggested length — a rough guide, not a limit. Write as much or as little as it takes to actually explain your thinking; a one-line answer to a 200-word question is fine if that's genuinely all there is to it. Plain text, voice memo transcribed, bullet points — whatever's fastest for you. Real past examples beat abstract descriptions every time.
